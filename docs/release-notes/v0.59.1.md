# Release notes — upcoming

## Reviews no longer open on a blank card

Starting a review on an argument that cites its sources used to begin with an
empty claim: no title, no body, just True / False / Unknown. On a typical cited
argument the very first step was one of these, and there were as many of them as
the argument had sources.

They were the sources themselves. An argument that backs a claim with a citation
records that citation as a claim of its own, and the review walk was collecting
every claim in the argument rather than the ones an author actually wrote. A
source carries no prose — its identity is the reference, not a sentence — so
there was nothing to put on the card. Axioms, the self-evident bases an argument
can rest a claim on, were being asked the same way, and an answer to one of
those was discarded rather than counted.

The walk now covers the claims the author wrote. Sources and axioms still do
their work in the argument; they are just no longer put to you as questions. A
cited argument's review is correspondingly shorter — the step count reflects the
claims you are being asked about, not the argument's total claim count.

Reviewing an argument that cites nothing is unchanged.
