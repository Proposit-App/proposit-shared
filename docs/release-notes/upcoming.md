# Release notes — upcoming

## A conclusion you settled both ways now says so, instead of reading "Unknown"

A review can reach a state where your own answers, run through the steps you
accepted, make a claim true and false at the same time — you assign a claim, and
then grant a step that concludes the other way. Until now the review had nowhere
to put that. It reported the conclusion as **Unknown**, which says the opposite
of what happened: unknown means nobody settled it, and this is a claim settled
twice, in opposite directions.

There is now a fourth value for it, **Contested**, with its own entry in the
explanatory material: what it means in plain words, a worked example, and
references for a reader who wants the formalism behind it. The definition says
the two things that matter — how it differs from Unknown, and that it is always
yours to resolve, because both halves came from you. Change one of the values
you assigned, or withdraw one of the steps you accepted, and it goes.

It is not a mark against the argument. A carefully built argument produces this
the moment a reader disagrees with it in a particular way, so the assessment of
the author's work is unchanged and reported separately, exactly as before.

## The same finding, under one name

A claim can also come out both ways because two of the things the engine tracks
behind it disagree. The previous release reported that case as unknown plus a
separate list of affected claims. It is the same finding as the one above and now
reports the same way — one value, one word, one explanation, rather than two
vocabularies for "you said it both ways".

Reviews that contain a collision are still stopped before they can be finished,
including the cases that previously reached the results screen unremarked.
