If the values I have assigned collide with a premise I accepted, I am told
before I can finish my review, and finishing waits until the collision is
settled.

The alert names the premise involved, spells out what accepting it commits me to
in that premise's own terms, and lists the values that collide — each named by
its claim's own words rather than a symbol. Where a value was worked out by the
argument instead of set by me, I am told which premise produced it and from
which values, so I am never asked to change something I did not choose.

Both ways out are offered and either settles it: reject the premise — the right
move when the step generally holds but not in this case — or change one of the
colliding assignments. The review is re-checked after each change, and I carry
on as soon as nothing collides.

The alert describes the conflict and nothing more. It reports that a resolution
is available, not that anyone got anything wrong.
