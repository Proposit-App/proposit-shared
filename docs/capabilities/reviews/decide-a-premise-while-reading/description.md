Reading an argument, I can accept or reject a premise's reasoning where I am,
without starting a review and walking every step. The premise header carries the
decision beside the premise it belongs to: accept it, or reject it and name the
reason from the same vocabulary a review uses.

This is the premise counterpart of setting a claim's assignment by reacting. It
records the same kind of judgment the review flow records, taken from the reading
surface instead of the wizard, and it survives a reload — the decision is mine
and it is stored, not a transient highlight.

Only a published version offers it, and only a premise with a step to decide: a
premise whose reasoning nothing can accept or reject says so rather than showing
controls that would do nothing. Rejecting is withholding the step, never a claim
that the premise is false, so nothing here reports the argument as bad or says I
got something wrong.
