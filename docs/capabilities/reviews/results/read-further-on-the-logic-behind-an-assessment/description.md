Under each assessment's explanation I find a short list of established references
on the idea behind it — validity and soundness, logical consequence, three-valued
logic, the material conditional, formal fallacies — so a reader who wants the
real account can go and read it.

The references are to recognised, freely readable sources rather than to
Proposit's own documentation, and each is labelled with what it covers so I can
tell which one answers my question. They open away from my review and leave it
exactly where it was.

The list is short and specific to the assessment I am looking at. It is an
invitation, never a prerequisite: the explanation and its example stand on their
own for a reader who does not follow any of them.
