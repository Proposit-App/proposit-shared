The assessments are worked out from my decisions, so the results tell me what
those decisions were. Every claim I assigned and every premise I decided is
listed in one place, each named in its own words, with the value or decision I
gave it — true, false or unknown for a claim; accepted or rejected for a premise.
Which of the two a row is, is never ambiguous, even though the lists run
together and share no vocabulary.

A step I passed over is shown as skipped and a step I never reached is shown as
undecided; neither is quietly reported as a value I chose.

From any row I can go straight back to that step and change my answer. Doing so
re-works the assessments from my new decisions, so what I read at the end always
matches what I actually said. A review I am only reading — someone else's, or my
own after finishing — shows the same record without offering to change it.
