# Review results

What a reader is shown once they reach the end of a review walkthrough. Every
entry here is realized on **both** clients — the web review wizard and the mobile
review flow — from the same assessment object and the same copy, so the two can
never word the same result differently.

The stage has two jobs, and the entries split along them.

**Report.** The two assessments ([the conclusion and the argument](tcw://C/reviews/results/see-the-conclusion-and-argument-assessments))
are the headline, backed by the record they were worked out from: [every decision
I made](tcw://C/reviews/results/see-and-correct-every-decision-i-made) and [how
each premise contributed](tcw://C/reviews/results/see-how-each-premise-contributed).
[Checking every possible assignment](tcw://C/reviews/results/check-every-possible-assignment)
asks the stronger question the reader's own values cannot answer.

**Teach.** A label a reader cannot interpret reports nothing, so each assessment
carries [an explanation](tcw://C/reviews/results/understand-what-each-assessment-means),
[a worked example in Proposit's own notation](tcw://C/reviews/results/see-a-worked-example-of-an-assessment),
and [somewhere to read further](tcw://C/reviews/results/read-further-on-the-logic-behind-an-assessment).
Reviewing an argument is most people's first encounter with a truth value or a
rejected inference; the results stage is where that is either made legible or
left as jargon.

Two rules bind everything here. The two axes stay two — no entry may recombine
them into a single grade of the argument. And nothing on this stage allocates
fault: it reports what follows from the reader's decisions, never that the
reader or the author got something wrong.

Outside this group: `../see-when-an-arguments-premises-contradict` (told at the
_start_ of a review, not at the end), `../resolve-a-contradiction-before-finishing`
(the gate that stands in place of the assessments while a review is blocked, and
governs finishing rather than reporting), and `../make-a-review-public` (sharing,
reachable from here but not of here).
