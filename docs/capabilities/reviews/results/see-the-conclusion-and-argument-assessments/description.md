At the conclusion of an argument I see two assessments, each answering a
different question, and never a single combined one.

The **conclusion assessment** is about the proposition: it tells me whether the
conclusion comes out true, false or unknown. Beside it I see up to two separate
statements about where that value came from — that I assigned it, and either
that the argument reaches it on its own or that it holds only because I assigned
it. They stay separate statements, never fused into one.

The **argument assessment** is about whether the author established the
conclusion: it tells me that the argument reaches its conclusion, doesn't reach
it, or has premises that contradict each other. When it doesn't reach it, I am
told why — the conclusion came from me, I rejected part of its reasoning, not
enough was settled, or its premises hold and its conclusion doesn't follow. The
premises I rejected are counted beside the assessment in a badge of their own
("2 premises rejected", or "2 constraints declined" for constraints) rather than
folded into the outcome, so an argument can reach its conclusion and still show
what I set aside.

The two are independent and can legitimately disagree — a conclusion I hold true
can sit beside an argument that doesn't reach it — and no surface shows one
contradicting the other. Both are computed from my current claim assignments and
the premises I accepted, and both update live as I change them.
