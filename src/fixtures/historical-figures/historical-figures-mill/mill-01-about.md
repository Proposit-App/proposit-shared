# About: On Liberty

On Liberty is a philosophical essay by John Stuart Mill, first published in 1859. It argues for the value of individual freedom against the pressures of both state power and social conformity, and it advances what is often called the harm principle: that the only purpose for which power can rightly be exercised over any member of a civilised community, against their will, is to prevent harm to others.

The excerpt reproduced here is Chapter II, "Of the Liberty of Thought and Discussion," in which Mill defends freedom of opinion and expression. He argues that silencing any opinion assumes one's own infallibility; that a silenced opinion may be true, or partly true; and that even a wholly true opinion, if it is never openly contested, hardens into a lifeless dogma held without real understanding. Free and open discussion, he concludes, is what allows a belief to be held as a living truth.

The essay remains a central text in liberal political philosophy and in debates over free speech. The text reproduced here is in the public domain.
